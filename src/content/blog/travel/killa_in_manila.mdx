<p>When I found out that I was going to have the opportunity to speak at the Inside Asian Gaming Academy Summit in Manila, I will admit that I was pretty nervous. When I saw the list of speakers, I couldn’t help but feel a bit of imposter syndrome, since it seemed like (a) I was the youngest speaker by about 25 years, (b) all of the speakers were C-suite executives, and (c) I am young, but I look even younger than I actually am. But I was in Hawaii when I found out - and when I told my family that I was going to have to cut my trip short and go to Manila, I told them that “I have no choice but to be a Killa in Manila.”</p>

<p>So about two weeks before the day I had to speak, I got my trip pulled together. I would fly from Hawaii to Portland for a wedding, and then from Portland to Manila, and then from Manila back to the Bay Area for another wedding. Talk about whiplash… jet lag is officially ruining my life.</p>

<p>I was so excited when I got to the conference. It reminded me of the beginning days when I started my PhD, before I became so jaded about academia. I was all excited to meet the big players, exchange ideas with some of the industry’s leaders, and learn new things from the sessions. It had been a long while since I felt that way about a conference, and it was a nice feeling.</p>

<p>On the second day of the conference I got to do my panel. I was so nervous that when I picked up the microphone to speak for the first time my voice cracked. But as the panel went on, I started feeling more comfortable and gaining confidence. When it was over, I felt like I still had more to say! I got really good feedback from a lot of the people in the crowd as well as the organizers of the conference. It was so good that IAG wanted to record a mini video interview of me at the conference for their “Trade Talk” bulletin!</p>

<p>The following day ended up being full of meetings - some of them set up by Clay (my boss) and some a result of the thought-provoking panel that I was able to be on! It was a bit of a hectic day Ubering from resort-to-resort, but it was really cool to be able to learn about the different properties and meet the people that are in charge of key initiatives.</p>

<p>The other really positive thing about the trip was that I got to hang out with my team in the Philippines. We hired Era, our project/product manager, about a year ago and she is just such a good human. And I also got to meet our new programmer, Khazel, in person as well. It was really nice to connect with them and I only wish that I could spend more time with them.</p>

<p>We went out to have dinner the three of us and Era and Khazel ordered me a surprise birthday cake at the end of the meal to celebrate my birthday coming up in about a week or so. I was totally oblivious to it, and I was so embarrassed when everyone in the restaurant started singing to me. But it was a really nice gesture and it felt like family time, except very far from home.</p>
