<p>One of the things that amazed me most when I first got to Macau was how visible superstition was in the casinos. There was no fourth floor. But it was not just four: no 14, no 24, no 34, and always no 40 through 49. It just kept going.</p>

<p>In line with that superstition is the Chinese baccarat player's love for the trend board. The way people play according to baccarat trends, you might think that the hands were not random at all, but did in fact follow some trends.</p>

<p>They do not. The previous hand does not tell the cards what to do next. But that does not make the trend board meaningless. If a pattern changes what players believe, where they place their money, and how much they wager, then it is behaviorally very real.</p>

<p>So, we took a look at some of the trends and who agreed with them. I matched the visible history before each hand to the side implied by that trend, then checked whether the player's actual wager agreed. Across the five common trends with the strongest agreement, this is what the road was telling the table.</p>

<p>The two five-hand streaks were the clearest signals. After five Bankers, 96.0% of wagers were on Banker again. After five Players, 94.5% were on Player. Even the mixed patterns attracted substantial agreement. Players were not simply counting which side had appeared most often; they were reading shapes and sequences.</p>

<p>But the result that stood out to me was what happened around four.</p>

<p>After three Bankers in a row, 71.4% of players bet Banker for the fourth hand, with an average wager of $903. Once the board already showed four Bankers, 98.0% bet Banker for the fifth, and the average wager rose to $988.</p>

<p>The Player side did almost the same thing: agreement jumped from 68.5% after three Players to 97.1% after four, while the average wager rose from $912 to $973. So this was not only the usual preference for Banker. Something about crossing that fourth result made the continuation feel much more convincing.</p>

<p class="fear-four-question">Are players hesitant to be the ones betting into the unlucky fourth result, but comfortable once four is safely behind them? Are they scared of four?</p>

<p class="fear-four-punchline">Maybe we should be.</p>

<p>That is the part I find most interesting. The cards are random, but the decisions around them are not. A trend board takes a sequence with no predictive power and gives it a shape, a name, and a story. And once the story looks convincing enough, nearly everyone at the table seems willing to agree with it.</p>
